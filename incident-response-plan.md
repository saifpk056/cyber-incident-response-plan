# 🛡️ Cyber Incident Response Plan

## 1. Purpose

This plan provides Internee.pk staff with a structured process for responding to cybersecurity incidents while protecting systems, data, employees, and business operations.

## 2. Scope

This plan applies to:

- Workstations and laptops
- Servers
- Cloud services
- Email systems
- Databases
- File storage
- Employee accounts
- Third-party services

## 3. Incident Severity

| Level | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Major operational or data impact | Ransomware affecting critical systems |
| High | Confirmed security compromise | Compromised employee account |
| Medium | Limited security event | Malware detected and blocked |
| Low | Suspicious activity without confirmed compromise | Suspicious phishing email |

## 4. Incident Response Roles

### Incident Lead

Coordinates the overall incident response and makes response decisions.

### IT/Security Team

Responsible for investigation, containment, eradication, monitoring, and recovery.

### Management

Approves important business decisions and communications.

### Communications/HR

Coordinates approved internal staff communications.

### Legal/Privacy Team

Assesses notification and reporting requirements when applicable.

### Employees

Report suspicious activity immediately and follow instructions from the security team.

## 5. Preparation

Before an incident occurs:

- Maintain an updated asset inventory.
- Maintain secure and tested backups.
- Enable security monitoring and logging.
- Keep operating systems and applications updated.
- Use strong authentication and MFA where available.
- Maintain emergency contact information.
- Define emergency communication channels.
- Conduct regular security awareness training.
- Perform incident response exercises.

## 6. Detection and Analysis

When a security alert or incident report is received:

1. Record the date and time.
2. Identify the reporting person and system.
3. Preserve relevant logs and evidence.
4. Identify potentially affected systems.
5. Identify potentially affected accounts.
6. Determine whether the event is a false positive or confirmed incident.
7. Assign an initial severity level.
8. Notify the Incident Lead.

Do not delete evidence or unnecessarily modify affected systems.

## 7. Containment

The purpose of containment is to prevent the incident from spreading.

For a suspected ransomware incident:

1. Isolate affected systems from the network.
2. Check whether additional systems are affected.
3. Protect unaffected systems.
4. Protect backup infrastructure.
5. Disable compromised accounts when appropriate.
6. Use an alternative communication method if normal communication systems may be affected.
7. Record all containment actions.

## 8. Eradication

After containment:

- Identify the likely initial access method.
- Remove malicious persistence.
- Reset compromised credentials.
- Patch exploited vulnerabilities.
- Remove unauthorized software.
- Rebuild compromised systems when appropriate.
- Verify security controls before reconnecting systems.

## 9. Recovery

Recovery should be performed in a controlled manner.

1. Identify critical business services.
2. Verify that clean backups are available.
3. Verify backup integrity.
4. Restore critical systems first.
5. Restore additional systems in stages.
6. Monitor restored systems.
7. Confirm normal business functionality.
8. Document recovery actions.

## 10. Communication

Internal communication should be:

- Accurate
- Brief
- Approved
- Need-to-know

Staff should receive clear instructions about what actions they need to take.

Do not speculate about attackers, data exposure, or ransom demands.

External communication should follow organizational policies and applicable legal requirements.

## 11. Evidence Preservation

Important evidence may include:

- Security logs
- Authentication logs
- Endpoint alerts
- Network logs
- Relevant email messages
- System timestamps
- Incident reports

Evidence should be handled carefully and access should be limited to authorized personnel.

## 12. Lessons Learned

After the incident:

- Build a complete incident timeline.
- Identify the root cause.
- Identify contributing factors.
- Review what worked.
- Identify what failed.
- Document security gaps.
- Assign corrective actions.
- Update security controls.
- Update staff training.
- Update the incident response plan.

## 13. Incident Closure

An incident can be closed when:

- The threat has been contained.
- Malicious activity has been removed.
- Critical systems have been recovered.
- Required evidence has been documented.
- Required notifications have been completed.
- Lessons learned have been recorded.
- Corrective actions have been assigned.

## 14. Success Criteria

The incident response process is successful when:

- Incidents are detected quickly.
- Affected systems are contained.
- Evidence is preserved.
- Critical services are recovered safely.
- Communication is coordinated.
- Corrective actions are completed.
- Security procedures are improved.
