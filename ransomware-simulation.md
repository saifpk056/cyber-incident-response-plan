# 🦠 Ransomware Incident Simulation

## 1. Exercise Type

This is a **non-destructive ransomware tabletop exercise** designed to train Internee.pk staff.

No real ransomware, malicious files, exploit code, or destructive commands are used.

---

## 2. Scenario

At 09:15, an employee reports that several project documents cannot be opened.

At the same time, the security monitoring system reports unusual file activity on the employee's workstation.

The security team begins investigating the incident.

A fictional ransom note is discovered during the exercise.

The response team must determine how to detect, contain, investigate, and recover from the simulated incident.

---

## 3. Exercise Objectives

The exercise is designed to test whether staff can:

- Detect suspicious activity.
- Report an incident quickly.
- Escalate the incident correctly.
- Isolate affected systems.
- Preserve evidence.
- Protect backups.
- Identify affected systems.
- Coordinate recovery.
- Communicate accurately.
- Document lessons learned.

# 5. Incident Handling Steps

## Step 1 — Detection

The employee reports that files cannot be opened.

The security team:

- Records the report time.
- Identifies the affected user.
- Identifies the affected workstation.
- Reviews available security alerts.
- Preserves relevant logs.

---

## Step 2 — Analysis

The security team determines:

- Which systems are affected.
- Which accounts are involved.
- Whether suspicious activity is continuing.
- Whether shared storage may be affected.
- Whether backup systems are safe.
- Whether the event should be classified as a Critical incident.

---

## Step 3 — Containment

The response team:

1. Isolates affected systems.
2. Checks for additional affected devices.
3. Protects unaffected systems.
4. Protects backup infrastructure.
5. Reviews compromised accounts.
6. Records all containment actions.

The objective is to prevent further spread.

---

## Step 4 — Evidence Preservation

The team preserves relevant information such as:

- Security logs
- Authentication logs
- Endpoint alerts
- Network logs
- Relevant email messages
- System timestamps
- Incident records

Evidence should only be handled by authorized personnel.

---

## Step 5 — Eradication

After containment, the security team:

- Investigates the likely initial access.
- Removes unauthorized persistence.
- Resets affected credentials.
- Patches vulnerabilities.
- Rebuilds compromised systems when appropriate.
- Verifies security controls.

---

## Step 6 — Recovery

The recovery team:

1. Identifies critical services.
2. Verifies clean backups.
3. Checks backup integrity.
4. Restores critical systems.
5. Restores additional systems in stages.
6. Monitors restored systems.
7. Confirms normal operation.

---

# 6. Communication Procedure

During the exercise:

### Employees

Employees should report the incident immediately.

### IT/Security Team

The security team provides verified technical information to the Incident Lead.

### Management

Management receives regular status updates and approves important business decisions.

### External Communication

External communication should only be performed through approved organizational procedures.

---

# 7. Exercise Questions

Participants should answer:

1. Who receives the first incident report?
2. Who declares the incident Critical?
3. How quickly can the affected device be isolated?
4. How are additional affected systems identified?
5. Where are the organization's backups stored?
6. How is backup integrity verified?
7. Which systems should be restored first?
8. What communication method is used if email is unavailable?
9. What evidence should be preserved?
10. Who approves external communications?

---

# 8. Expected Outcome

At the end of the exercise, participants should demonstrate that they can:

- Detect the simulated incident.
- Escalate the incident.
- Contain affected systems.
- Protect backups.
- Preserve evidence.
- Coordinate recovery.
- Communicate verified information.
- Document lessons learned.

---

# 9. Lessons Learned

After the exercise, the response team should document:

- What worked well?
- What caused delays?
- Were responsibilities clear?
- Were backups available?
- Was communication effective?
- Were security tools sufficient?
- What procedures need improvement?
- What additional staff training is required?

---

# 10. Safety Boundary

This exercise is strictly for defensive cybersecurity training.

Use:

- Fictional data
- Authorized test systems
- Controlled environments
- Non-destructive simulations

Do not execute real ransomware or intentionally encrypt organizational data.

---

## 11. Final Result

The ransomware simulation demonstrates a complete defensive response:

**Detect → Analyze → Contain → Eradicate → Recover → Learn**
